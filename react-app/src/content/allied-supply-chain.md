A US defense startup building humanoids or UAVs has three hard constraints. It has to **move fast** to clear Pentagon procurement before the window closes. It has to be **NDAA-compliant**, with no banned parts anywhere in the bill of materials. And it has to keep **BOM cost** low enough to win on price at scale. Today, you can pick two.

## The Opportunity Is Now

Humanoid robots and unmanned aerial vehicles are the next two embodied agents scaling into the trillions. Goldman Sachs puts humanoid TAM at $38B by 2035; Morgan Stanley's "Humanoid 100" projects $5T+ by 2050. UAVs are already a $90B+ market, with the military segment alone reaching $31B by 2032.

This is not hypothetical. Ukraine has industrialized drone warfare at over 10,000 FPV losses a month. The Pentagon's Replicator initiative is fielding thousands of attritable autonomous systems. Apptronik is testing humanoids with the US Army; Anduril is shipping ALTIUS, Bolt-M, and Roadrunner. **The procurement is happening right now.**

## The China Wall

Almost every component inside a humanoid or a UAV is built in China - harmonic reducers, BLDC motors, lithium cells, image sensors, motor drivers, and the long tail of analog ICs. China refines roughly 85% of the world's rare earths. DJI alone holds ~70% of the global commercial drone market.

NDAA §889, §5949, §1260H, and the American Security Drone Act make that a dead end for anyone selling to the Pentagon. A single Chinese-sourced part voids the contract. No waiver, no workaround, no roadmap to compliance for a bill of materials rooted in Shenzhen.

## Reshoring Is the 2030s Answer

The right long-term answer is to build the capability at home - the same lesson we learned with semiconductors. But standing up a domestic Shenzhen takes years of capex and decades of supplier depth. That's a serious project for the next decade, not an answer for a startup chasing a contract this year. **Speed and sovereignty pull in opposite directions - unless there's a third option.**

## Japan Is NDAA-Compliant by Default

There is one: source from a treaty ally that already manufactures every category of part, at a fraction of US cost, with statutory NDAA eligibility on day one.

Harmonic Drive Systems. Nidec. Mabuchi. NSK. NTN. JTEKT. Sony. Murata. Hokuyo. Panasonic. Renesas. Decades-old leaders in precision components, manufacturing 25-40% below US-equivalent cost. DICAS (2024) and Japan's revised Three Principles on Defense Equipment Transfer cleared the legal path; in April 2026, Japan scrapped the "five categories" rule that had restricted lethal defense exports outright. In a Nikkei interview, Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi openly called on US defense startups and their VC backers to engage Japan's industrial base, noting that "American manufacturing has weakened, and the US is now seeking cooperation from allies."

Japan resolves the trilemma: fast, compliant, and cheap at the same time.

## The Missing Piece

The supply chain already exists. What's missing is reliable access to it. A US founder doesn't have the relationships, the language, or the export-control fluency to walk into Harmonic Drive or Murata and come out with a qualified, contract-ready BOM. That bridge between American defense startups and the Japanese industrial base hasn't been built yet.

**The brains are American. The muscle can be allied. Someone just has to connect them.**
